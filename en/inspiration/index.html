Why order matters · October 2026

What clutter does to us

A selection of Danish articles on tidying, stress and everyday life, summed up briefly with a link to the source (in Danish).

Stress

Clutter makes us stressed

In a TV 2 poll, 64 per cent said they get stressed and irritable when things are cluttered. The tidying expert advises taking one room at a time, ideally with help, and sorting out before you organise.

Read the source: TV MIDTVEST ↗

Family life

A family in mid-Jutland found calm

The house was emptied, and among the things were 837 new items that had never been used. Today everything has a fixed place, stress is down and the family buys less. Five minutes of tidying beats none.

Read the source: TV 2 Livsstil ↗

Family life

Now the friends come round again

A 16-year-old avoided inviting friends home because the flat was full, and his mother had slept on the sofa for years. After the clear-out they have guests again. Clutter affects children’s social lives too.

Read the source: TV 2 Livsstil ↗

Shame and pressure

A home may look lived in

A mother of three shows her messy home to push back against the pressure of the perfect home. A researcher from Aarhus University explains that many feel clutter as a loss of control, which can bring shame.

Read the source: TV 2 Østjylland ↗

Good advice

Start with one cupboard

A tidying expert sorts things into favourites, filler and things that drain energy, like clothes that no longer fit. Start in one place, such as the wardrobe. Many people don’t know you can get professional help.

Read the source: TV 2 Kosmopol ↗

Life stages

Clear out while you can

Swedish death cleaning means sorting your things in good time so your family is spared. A psychotherapist says the method also frees up space for people in mid-life. Agree in advance who gets what.

Read the source: TV 2 Nyheder ↗

Workplace

Digital stress at work

A researcher at Roskilde University describes how emails, messages and overlapping systems take time from core tasks and leave us more tired. Clear agreements and a fixed structure ease the pressure.

Read the source: A4 Medier ↗

Workplace

A cluttered inbox stresses too

A work psychologist explains that an overflowing inbox can cause the same unease as physical clutter. The fear of missing something triggers the body’s stress response. Order in folders and mail brings calm.

Read the source: DR ↗
